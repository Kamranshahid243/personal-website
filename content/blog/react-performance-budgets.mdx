Performance advice dies in slide decks. Budgets survive when they are small,
owned, and enforced where code merges.

## Pick budgets that map to money or trust

- **LCP** on the marketing and product entry routes
- **INP** on the flows people click repeatedly
- **JS weight** on the client bundles that ship to every visitor

If a metric cannot be tied to a user outcome, it will be negotiated away.

## Measure in CI and in the field

Lab scores catch obvious regressions. RUM tells you whether a mid-tier Android
device on LTE still feels the product you designed on a MacBook.

## Attack the critical path, not the average component

Profile the route. Delete or defer the work on the first interaction. Memo
theatre after the fact rarely moves the needle.

## Make the budget visible

A failing check with a clear owner beats a dashboard nobody opens. Pair the
number with the PR that broke it.
